BSF Larval Growth & Derivatives $f'(t)$

Optimizing insect protein bioconversion rates to replace costly imported soybean meal in Malaysian animal feed formulations.

National Target

Targeting 100% feed self-sufficiency

The Food Security Problem

Malaysia imports over 70% of its animal feed ingredients (specifically corn and soy), leaving the country highly vulnerable to global supply chain disruptions and currency fluctuations.

Black Soldier Fly Larvae (BSFL) feed on local organic waste, converting it into high-protein biomass. To make commercial bio-factories viable, agronomists must know the exact day of maximum growth velocity. Harvesting too early loses yield; harvesting too late (when they turn into prepupae and shed fat) reduces protein content.

Realistic Baseline Source:

"Bioconversion of Organic Waste via Hermetia illucens" — MARDI (Malaysian Agricultural Research and Development Institute) Technical Guidelines / Anim. Feed Sci. Technology.

Biological Calculus Models

Let $t$ represent the growth cycle in **Days** ($2 \le t \le 18$), and $f(t)$ represent the **average larval wet weight in milligrams (mg)**.

Biomass Accumulation Curve $f(t)$
$$f(t) = -0.05t^3 + 1.2t^2 - 2t + 5$$
Derivative $f'(t)$ (Growth Rate)
$$f'(t) = -0.15t^2 + 2.4t - 2$$
Unit: milligrams / day
